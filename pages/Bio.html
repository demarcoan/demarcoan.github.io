I like to build and measure things.

I am a PhD student in Soft Matter Physics and Microfluidics, currently focusing on how complex fluids evolve, flow, and interact with their surroundings under confinement and in heterogeneous microstructured environments.
In this broad context, my work spans both fundamental soft-matter systems and applied microfluidic platforms, including flow-based technologies for energy storage.

I combine experimental techniques such as Differential Dynamic Microscopy and Dynamic Light Scattering with computational analysis and modeling. A central part of my work is the development of quantitative tools to extract dynamical information from images and connect it to physical quantities.

I also enjoy building the experimental setups I work with, taking advantage of custom-designed devices tailored to specific physical questions.
